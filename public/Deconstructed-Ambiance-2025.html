Deconstructed Ambiance2025

A generative light object exploring high- and low-dimensional spaces in machine learning.

Exhibited at ‘Beyond Interface & Prompt: Exploring the Language of Technology in Contemporary Art’ at the Williamsburg Art & Historical Center, Brooklyn, New York (18 April–17 May 2025)

Part of a series of work exploring Machine Learning and Light — other works in series Reconstructed Colours (2026), Ambi(latent) Space (2025) and Deconstructed Colours (2023).

Deconstructed Ambiance (2025) is a generative light object that explores the relationship between the machine learning concepts of high- and low-dimensional spaces, reinterpreting light as a representation of a latent space.

In machine learning, a high-dimensional space refers to a space that has many variables or features, while a low-dimensional space has fewer. In Deconstructed Ambiance, the screen’s colours, which sit in a high-dimensional space, are translated into the low-dimensional space of a single ambient light, so the latent space is read in the atmosphere of the room rather than on a screen.

The work processes its data through a layered chain of software and hardware. An Arduino and LCD screen cycle through random colours to provide the input for the installation. Another Arduino uses its light spectrum sensor to pick up these colours and translate them into separate red, green, and blue values that control the LEDs with varying intensities and a fading animation. The LEDs then reconstruct the visual wavelength of colour through light.
