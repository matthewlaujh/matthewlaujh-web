Reconstructed Colours2026

A generative light object that learns from its own output until its colours collapse.

Exhibited at ‘Techno Terra: Archive of the Future’ at Gallery 1, Institute of Contemporary Arts Singapore, LASALLE College of the Arts (4 September–24 October 2026)

Part of a series of work exploring Machine Learning and Light — other works in series Ambi(latent) Space (2025), Deconstructed Ambiance (2025) and Deconstructed Colours (2023).

As our understanding of machine learning concepts is often limited, Reconstructed Colours (2026) is a generative light object that explores what happens when a system is left to learn from itself. Continuing from Deconstructed Colours (2023), the work closes the circuit: colours perceived by the installation are fed back to the screen, perceived again, and learned again. Through the installation, viewers are encouraged to contemplate what survives when a system has nothing to learn from but its own output.

In machine learning, a model trained on data it produced itself will gradually narrow. Rare values disappear first, variety shrinks, and the range of what the model can produce collapses toward a single average. This is known as model collapse, and how quickly it happens depends on the proportion of recycled data to fresh. In Reconstructed Colours that proportion is a controllable value, and the work is tuned through it.

A screen resolves each colour out of noise, laying it down as a square over those before it, so earlier colours remain visible wherever a new one falls short of covering them. A spectral sensor reads what it finds there as nine separate wavelength bands, and software arranges every reading as a point in a living two-dimensional plot, rendered across a suspended panel of light that hangs face-down and casts into the room below. A second sensor sits among those lights, reading what they emit, and returns it to the screen to be displayed again — so most of what the system perceives is something it has already produced. Points arrive with a ripple, gather with their spectral relatives, age, dim, and evaporate, so structure has to be continuously re-earned from new data. As the recycled proportion rises, the palette narrows, the screen darkens as its source starves, and the field condenses into a single dense mass before sinking to an ember, until a fresh outlier reseeds it. Over enough passes, the colour the system settles on is not one the screen ever showed. It is a yellow-green belonging to the apparatus itself.
